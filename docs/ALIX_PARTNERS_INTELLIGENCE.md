## 2. What they are publicly telling their clients (their agenda)

### 2026 Enterprise Software Technology Predictions report
- **AI accelerates software development by 20–30%** — but companies struggle to convert those gains into profit. They call this **"The AI Productivity Paradox."**
- Their prescription: enterprises must **invest in trust infrastructure** — 10–15% of budgets in 2025, scaling to **20–30% by 2027**.
- Jake Cantu (AlixPartners): *"The winners will be those who execute bold operational transformation, not just adopt new tools."*

### 2026 AlixPartners Disruption Index
- **80% of CEOs are optimistic** about AI's impact — but adoption pace is **"the great divider"**: **51% of growth leaders** have widely implemented agentic AI vs **14% of slower-growing companies**.
- Top obstacles their clients report: cultural resistance (43%), budget (41%), **lack of clarity or consensus (39%)**, talent (31%).
- Rob Hornby (Co-CEO): *"Monetizing AI and driving real tangible results requires greater focus and prioritization."*

### Their AI practice
- Positioning: **"Turning AI into ROI"** — *"decades of business experience, sharp human judgment, and deep technological expertise to unlock quantifiable results at pace."*
- They **build and deliver AI solutions for clients using Claude** (Anthropic partnership) — meaning AI-generated code quality is not just their clients' problem; it is **their own delivery-risk problem** on live engagements.
- They maintain a cybersecurity consulting practice — security-density evidence lands with an existing service line.

## 4. How to speak their language in the room
- Open the problem slide **with their own numbers**, not ours. Nothing is more persuasive to a consultant than their firm's published research.
- Say **"trust infrastructure"** — it is their term; using it makes the platform legible as the thing they already told the market to buy.
- Never position against SonarQube defensively; position as **the evidence layer their advisory work currently lacks**.
- For Ollie: every metric maps to a governance criterion and a client conversation. For Paul: every number is deterministic, provenance-stamped, and reproducible.
- Close on their ground: this is what assurance looks like **"when it really matters."**
